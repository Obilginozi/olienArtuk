The table

How to pour it.

Firm enough for slow meat and winter cooking. A quieter hand for the grill. Always as a finishing touch — never the cooking medium. Raw beef and fatty fish belong to Carpaccio.

Slow meat

Beef cheek, lamb, long braises. A robust, peppery finish.

Winter table

Root vegetables, thick soups, and structured reds that carry the oil.

Grill

A quieter pour on grilled cuts: quality first, less intensity on the plate.

For slow-cooked cuts, winter tables, and a glass of red.

In the kitchen

Finish the plate. Do not cook the meat in the oil.

A last pour keeps the character of the dish intact. Heat softens what the lot was pressed to show.

The standard

Every lot, not every label.

Variety names alone do not define intensity. Each Red lot is written to sensory scores and laboratory freshness — then sealed in dark glass.

Sensory

Freshness

Protection

Dark glass against light and oxygen. Store cool, away from heat.
