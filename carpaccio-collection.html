The profile

What you taste.

Medium fruit on the light side of the band. Green, herbaceous, and almond-leaning — with bitterness and pungency held at 3.0–4.0 so cold plates stay vivid, not overwhelmed.

The table

How to pour it.

For raw and thin-sliced plates that need aroma without weight. White fish and scallop carpaccio stay with White Wine. Cooked red meat stays with Red.

Raw beef

Cold sirloin, thinly sliced. A mid pour that finishes the plate — never a marinade.

Tuna & salmon

Fatty raw fish that can carry balanced bitterness and clean green fruit.

Vegetables & fruit

Soft vegetables and fruit carpaccio. Herbaceous notes that stay articulate when cold.

For cold slices — beef, fatty fish, vegetables, and fruit.

In the kitchen

Last pour on cold slices. Do not marinate.

Carpaccio is finishing oil, not a cooking medium. Defects have nowhere to hide. Pour at the last moment so aroma still reads on a cold plate.

The standard

Every lot, not every label.

Unflavoured extra virgin — written to sensory scores and laboratory freshness, then sealed for the cold table. Variety names alone never write the mid rung.

Sensory

Defect-free extra virgin. Fruit 3.5–5.0; bitterness and pungency 3.0–4.0, well balanced.

Freshness

Lot analysis for freshness markers. DAG ≥ 35%, PPP ≤ 17%. Chemistry kept inside trade limits.

Protection

Protected glass against light and oxygen. Store cool — open bottles do not wait on aroma.
