The principle

Match the dish. Then the glass.

A firmer oil for cooked red meat. A mid pour for cold, thin slices. A lighter oil for the sea. Each lot is written for the table first.

At the table

Oil, spoken plainly.

From Michelin kitchens to the critic’s first bite — the same idea keeps returning: oil belongs on the table, not as an afterthought.

Gordon Ramsay

In his Business Traveller interview, Gordon Ramsay put olive oil ahead of butter for how he eats and feels:

I’ll take a tablespoon of olive oil over five knobs of butter any day.

Business Traveller

Gordon Ramsay

Talking to Good Food, he described his daughter Tilly arriving at a friend’s birthday with oil instead of wine:

I was looking at Tilly last week going to a friend’s 25th birthday party and she’s taking a litre of olive oil. And that’s how they want to live.
